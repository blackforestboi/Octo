# Octo Website Planning Brief

Created: 2026-07-29

## Purpose

Plan a focused marketing website for Octo, the macOS voice-to-text menu bar app. The site should quickly make the product's time-saving value tangible, establish trust around privacy and on-device transcription, and guide interested visitors toward downloading or learning more about Octo.

This is a content, design, and launch plan only. It intentionally does not prescribe a technical stack, create designs, write production copy beyond the approved hero line, or implement a website.

## Working Assumptions

- The initial launch is a single-page marketing site for prospective macOS users.
- The primary conversion is downloading Octo; any secondary conversion, such as joining a waitlist or reading documentation, should not compete with it visually.
- The site will accurately reflect Octo's existing product capabilities and avoid unverified performance, privacy, or compatibility claims.
- The voice should be concise, confident, and practical rather than overly technical or productivity-cliché driven.

## Audience

| Audience | Need | Message to validate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Knowledge workers | Reduce the time spent writing messages, notes, and drafts | Speaking is materially faster than typing for everyday work. |
| Developers and technical users | A fast tool that fits into an existing desktop workflow | Octo lives in the menu bar, works with hotkeys, and keeps the workflow lightweight. |
| Privacy-conscious Mac users | Confidence that speech data is handled responsibly | Transcription happens on device; the exact wording must be reviewed against the product's current behavior. |

## Core Positioning

Octo turns spoken thoughts into usable text on a Mac without making voice input feel like a separate workflow. The story should begin with recovered time, then substantiate that promise through the product experience, on-device processing, and simple activation.

### Approved Hero Direction

**Tagline:** “You spend twenty hours per week typing for what you could say in two.”

Use this as the primary header statement, with enough visual space to let the contrast land. The surrounding copy should clarify that the comparison is an aspirational productivity framing, not a measured guarantee. Do not attach unsupported numerical claims elsewhere on the page.

## Proposed Page Structure

1. **Hero**
   - Present the tagline, a one-sentence explanation of Octo, a product visual, and a primary download call to action.
   - Include a compact macOS compatibility or availability note only after it is verified.

2. **The before-and-after workflow**
   - Show a familiar typing-heavy moment alongside the equivalent spoken workflow.
   - Make the benefit concrete: drafting a message, capturing a thought, or writing a note without breaking focus.

3. **How Octo works**
   - Explain the short interaction loop: trigger hotkey, speak, receive text, continue working.
   - Use three concise steps supported by an authentic product screenshot or short demonstration.

4. **Why it feels safe and fast**
   - Explain on-device transcription and the menu-bar-first experience in plain language.
   - Treat model choice, accuracy, language support, and privacy claims as facts requiring product review before publication.

5. **Feature proof**
   - Highlight only capabilities that are already available at launch: configurable hotkeys, transcription models, automatic paste, and history if those claims are confirmed.
   - Prefer specific examples over a long feature grid.

6. **Download close**
   - Repeat the primary download action with a short, low-friction reassurance.
   - Add links to support, privacy, and release notes once those destinations are ready.

## Content Principles

- Lead with outcomes, then explain the mechanism.
- Keep each section answerable in a quick scan: what it does, why it matters, and what to do next.
- Use product screenshots and demonstrations that show real text and controls without exposing sensitive content.
- Avoid comparisons to named competitors unless they are accurate, useful, and legally reviewed.
- Make accessibility part of content production: descriptive alt text, captions for motion, sufficient contrast, and no meaning conveyed by color alone.

## Visual Direction

- Make Octo's macOS-native, unobtrusive character visible through generous spacing and focused interface imagery.
- Let the hero headline carry the emotional weight; supporting visuals should demonstrate the product rather than decorate it.
- Use motion sparingly to illustrate the speak-to-text flow, with a static equivalent for reduced-motion preferences.
- Establish one recognizable visual motif for spoken input becoming text, then repeat it consistently across the page.

## Delivery Phases

| Phase | Outcome | Key decisions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Product and message validation | A factual messaging brief | Confirm audience priority, download destination, supported platforms, and approved privacy/model claims. |
| 2. Content and information architecture | Final section outline and draft copy | Decide which workflows and features earn space on the page. |
| 3. Visual design | Responsive page design and asset list | Choose hero treatment, screenshots, demonstration format, and accessibility treatment. |
| 4. Build and integration | Production website implementation | Select hosting, analytics, download delivery, and support/legal destinations. |
| 5. Launch review | Ready-to-publish site | Verify copy, links, performance, accessibility, consent needs, and conversion tracking. |

## Open Decisions Before Design Begins

- What is the exact primary call to action and where does it send visitors?
- Is Octo free, paid, trial-based, or invite-only at launch?
- Which privacy and on-device claims have been approved word-for-word?
- Which product demonstrations best represent the intended audience's daily work?
- What supporting pages must exist at launch: privacy policy, support, documentation, changelog, or press kit?
- What outcome defines success for the first version: downloads, sign-ups, qualified interest, or another measure?

## Scope Boundaries

### In scope for the first website plan

- A concise public marketing experience for Octo.
- Product messaging, page hierarchy, visual direction, asset needs, and launch-readiness decisions.
- The provided hero tagline as the initial creative anchor.

### Deferred for later

- Blog, extensive documentation, comparison pages, customer stories, pricing experiments, and localization.
- A separate web application, account system, or in-browser transcription product.
- Implementation choices, tracking configuration, and search-engine optimization details.

## Definition of a Ready-to-Build Brief

- The hero statement, supporting value proposition, and primary action are approved.
- Every factual claim has a product owner or source of truth.
- The page outline has a defined purpose and call to action for every section.
- Required screenshots, demos, legal copy, and destination URLs are identified.
- The first-launch success metric and review checklist are agreed before implementation begins.
